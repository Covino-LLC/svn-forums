## PLANT 22 — Biosphere
### Should a free citizen be guaranteed at least what a prisoner gets?

In 1976 the Supreme Court said that because a prisoner can't care for himself, the government must provide medical care. Prisons also provide meals and a bed. The federal Bureau of Prisons puts the average cost at $47,162 per inmate for 2024, about $129 a day.

A free person who loses a job or gets sick has no such guarantee. So here's the idea: every citizen gets at least that floor. That means food, shelter, and basic health care, plus free public transportation so people can get to work and appointments.

The simplest way to deliver it may be a check sent straight to each person. There is some evidence. In Stockton, California, 125 people got $500 a month. In the first year, full-time work among them rose from 28% to 40%, compared with 32% to 37% for a comparison group. In a larger study, 1,000 people got $1,000 a month for three years. They worked about 1 to 2 fewer hours a week, and the early gains in well-being faded.

The catch: cost, who qualifies, and whether checks or direct services work better. A floor also costs money that taxpayers have to approve.

So where should the floor be, and should it be cash, services, or both?
